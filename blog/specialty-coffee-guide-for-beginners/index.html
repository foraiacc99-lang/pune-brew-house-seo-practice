Specialty Coffee Guide for Beginners: Demystifying Third-Wave Coffee

By Arjun Kulkarni • Published: September 14, 2026 • 9 min read

If you've ever walked into a modern coffee shop and felt slightly intimidated by talk of "washed varietals," "V60 pour-overs," and "notes of stone fruit," you are not alone. For years, the world of specialty coffee has occasionally suffered from an image problem: a bit too technical, a bit too serious, and sometimes downright snobbish.

At Pune Brew House, we believe great coffee should be welcoming to everyone. You don't need a palate trained in cupping to enjoy a great cup. In this guide, we break down what specialty coffee actually means, why it tastes different from commercial coffee, and how to order with confidence.

1. What Does "Specialty Coffee" Actually Mean?

"Specialty coffee" is not just a marketing buzzword. It is a strict technical definition established by the Specialty Coffee Association (SCA).

Coffee beans are graded on a 100-point scale by certified sensory experts known as Q-Graders. To qualify as specialty grade:

  • The coffee must score 80 points or higher based on fragrance, flavor, acidity, body, balance, and sweetness.
  • It must have zero primary defects (such as black or sour rotten beans) and fewer than five secondary defects per 350-gram sample.
  • It must be 100% Arabica grown under specific climatic conditions, usually at elevations exceeding 1,000 meters above sea level.

Commercial coffee (the beans used in supermarket pre-ground tins and instant powder) generally scores below 75 points and consists of mixed harvest lots roasted extremely dark to mask defects.

2. The Three Waves of Coffee: A Quick History

  • First Wave (1950s–1980s): Coffee was a convenient household staple. Think instant coffee jars, pre-ground cans, and convenience above all else.
  • Second Wave (1990s–2000s): The rise of dark Italian roast cafe chains (Starbucks, Cafe Coffee Day, Costa). Drinks were defined by heavy syrup flavors, whipped cream, and cozy meeting spaces.
  • Third Wave (2010s–Present): Coffee is treated as an artisanal agricultural product, similar to fine wine or single-malt whiskey. Terroir, processing, roasting freshness, and direct farm trade are celebrated.

3. Do They Add Flavors? Decoding "Tasting Notes"

When a menu states that a coffee tastes like "Jasmine, crisp green apple, and raw honey," no apples or flowers were added!

Coffee cherries absorb organic minerals and sugars from the soil, rain, and shade trees they grow beneath. A light-roast washed coffee naturally retains organic malic acid (the same acid found in green apples) and floral esters. When roasted with care, these natural flavor compounds become prominent.

4. How to Order Confidently at Any Specialty Cafe

Next time you stand in front of a specialty counter, simply follow this 4-step framework:

  1. Do you want milk or black?
    • If milk: Choose based on strength. Want strong coffee flavor? Ask for a Cortado or Flat White. Want mild and creamy? Ask for a Cafe Latte.
    • If black: Want rich and bold? Choose an Americano. Want clean, tea-like, and refreshing? Ask for a V60 Pour-over.
  2. Hot or Iced?
    • During warm Pune afternoons, try an Espresso Tonic or an 18-Hour Kyoto Cold Drip.
  3. What dairy preference?
    • Specialty cafes now offer oat milk and almond milk, which froth smoothly and complement coffee acidity.

Ask Your Barista!

Our baristas at Pune Brew House are here to guide you, not judge. Tell us what flavors you usually like (e.g., "I like dark chocolate and low acid," or "I want something bright and fruity"), and we will recommend the perfect brew. Explore our Drink Menu or visit us in Koregaon Park.
