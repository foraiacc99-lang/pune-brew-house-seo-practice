Coffee vs Cold Brew: What's the Difference? Extraction, Acidity & Chemistry

By Arjun Kulkarni • Published: September 5, 2026 • 7 min read

One of the most frequent questions our baristas answer at Pune Brew House is: "Isn't cold brew just regular iced coffee with ice cubes?" The short answer is an emphatic no. While both are chilled, cold brew and traditional iced coffee are created through fundamentally different chemical extractions.

Understanding how temperature and time interact transforms how you choose your coffee, especially if you experience stomach sensitivity or desire a naturally sweet afternoon pick-me-up. Let's dissect the differences across chemistry, acidity, flavor, and caffeine content.

1. Extraction Chemistry: Hot Water vs Cold Time

Brewing coffee is fundamentally an extraction of chemical compounds (solubles) from roasted coffee bean cell walls into water.

  • Hot Brewed Coffee: Uses near-boiling water (90°C to 96°C) to dissolve coffee solids in 25 seconds (espresso) to 4 minutes (pour-over / French press). Heat accelerates molecular motion, instantly extracting aromatic oils, fruit acids, and delicate volatile compounds.
  • Cold Brew: Uses cold or room-temperature water (4°C to 20°C) and replaces thermal energy with time (typically 14 to 20 hours). Because cold water lacks the thermal capacity to extract certain bitter tannins and oils, they remain trapped inside the coffee grounds.

2. Acidity and Stomach Friendliness

When hot water contacts roasted coffee, chlorogenic acids degrade into quinic and caffeic acids, which contribute to crisp tartness in a good cup, but can also trigger acid reflux or heartburn in sensitive coffee drinkers.

Scientific testing shows that cold brew has approximately 50% to 65% lower titratable acidity than hot coffee brewed with the identical bean. The result is a cup that is remarkably smooth, velvety, and easy on the stomach, even when consumed on an empty stomach.

3. Side-by-Side Comparison: Cold Brew vs Iced Americano

Feature Hot-Brewed Iced Coffee Slow-Drip Cold Brew
Water Temperature 90°C – 96°C (Chilled over ice) 4°C – 18°C (Cold steep)
Extraction Duration 2 to 4 minutes 14 to 18 hours
Perceived Acidity Bright, crisp, citrusy Very low, round, gentle
Flavor Profile Floral, fruit clarity, sharp finish Dark chocolate, molasses, dried dates
Refrigerated Shelf Life 2 to 4 hours (oxidizes quickly) 10 to 14 days in sealed glass

4. Caffeine Content: Which Packs a Stronger Punch?

Cold brew is frequently labeled as "super-caffeinated," but this depends on whether you drink it as a concentrate or diluted.

Because cold brew uses a higher coffee-to-water ratio (often 1:8 or 1:10 compared to 1:16 for hot drip), the resulting liquid concentrate has significantly more caffeine per milliliter. However, when diluted 1:1 with water, milk, or tonic, an 8oz glass of cold brew has roughly 160–200mg of caffeine—comparable to a large hot drip coffee or a double-shot Americano.

5. Which One Should You Choose?

Choose Hot or Iced Pour-Over when: You are drinking a delicate floral single-origin (e.g. Ethiopian Yirgacheffe or high-elevation Washed Chikkamagaluru) and want to experience the full spectrum of aromatic fruit acids.

Choose Cold Brew when: You want a smooth, chocolatey, low-acid drink that provides clean energy throughout a warm workday without jarring bitterness.
